🏛️ Module 1: Judiciary in the Constitution of India

Articles 124–147 (Supreme Court), Articles 214–231 (High Courts), and the 5 Prerogative Writs.

15 Marks Weightage
Part 1: Composition, Appointment & Impeachment Articles 124 – 130

• Article 124: Establishment of the Supreme Court. Originally had 1 CJI + 7 Judges (Total 8 in 1950). Parliament has the exclusive power to alter court strength via ordinary legislation (current strength: 34 Judges including the CJI).

• Eligibility Criteria for SC Judge: Must be a Citizen of India AND satisfy any one of these three conditions:

  • Has been a Judge of a High Court (or successive HCs) for at least 5 years; OR
  • Has been an Advocate of a High Court for at least 10 years; OR
  • Is, in the opinion of the President, a distinguished jurist (Note: A distinguished jurist can be appointed to the Supreme Court, but NOT to a High Court!).

• Tenure & Resignation: Holds office until age 65 years. Resignation must be addressed in writing to the President of India.

• Removal Process (Art 124(4) & Judges Inquiry Act 1968): Can ONLY be removed on two grounds: (1) Proven misbehaviour or (2) Incapacity. Motion requires 100 Lok Sabha or 50 Rajya Sabha signatures. If admitted, the Speaker/Chairman forms a 3-Member Inquiry Committee consisting of: (a) a Supreme Court Judge, (b) a Chief Justice of a High Court, and (c) a distinguished jurist. Only if this committee finds the judge guilty does Parliament vote. Requires an address by each House passed by a Special Majority (majority of total membership + at least 2/3 of members present and voting in the same session).

• Article 126: Acting CJI is appointed by the President when the office of CJI is vacant or CJI is unable to perform duties.

• Article 127: Ad-hoc Judges: Appointed by the Chief Justice of India (NOT the President!), with prior consent of the President and consultation with the Chief Justice of the concerned High Court, when there is lack of quorum.

• Article 129: Court of Record: Its judgments have evidentiary value and cannot be questioned in lower courts. It possesses the inherent power to punish for contempt of itself.

• Article 130: Seat of the Supreme Court: Delhi. The CJI has power to appoint another place or places with the prior approval of the President.

⚠️ Examiner's Trap #1: Who Appoints Ad-hoc Judges?

Candidates instinctively pick "President of India". Wrong! Under Article 127, Ad-hoc judges are appointed by the Chief Justice of India with the previous consent of the President.

Part 2: The Jurisdictions of the Supreme Court Articles 131 – 143
Jurisdiction Article Scope & Trigger Key Exceptions / Exclusions
Original Federal Jurisdiction Art 131 Exclusive disputes between: (a) Centre vs One or more States; (b) Centre + State(s) vs other State(s); (c) State vs State. Excludes: Pre-constitution treaties, inter-state water disputes (Art 262), Finance Commission references, private citizen suits.
Appellate Jurisdiction Arts 132–134 Appeals from High Courts on Constitutional (132), Civil (133), and Criminal (134) matters on certificate under Art 134A. Criminal appeal lies as of right if HC reversed acquittal and sentenced accused to death.
Special Leave Petition (SLP) Art 136 Extraordinary, discretionary power to grant leave to appeal against ANY judgment, decree, sentence or order from ANY court or tribunal. Absolute Bar: Cannot hear appeals against judgments passed by Military Tribunals / Courts Martial (Art 136(2)).
Review Jurisdiction Art 137 Power to review its own judgments/orders. Must be filed within 30 days. Heard by circulation in chambers by the same bench.
Curative Petition Inherent / Art 142 Final judicial remedy after review dismissal. Evolved in Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002) to cure gross miscarriage of justice. Must be certified by a Senior Advocate; circulated to 3 senior-most judges + original bench.
Complete Justice Art 142 Plenary power to pass any decree or order necessary for doing "complete justice" in any cause or matter. Cannot be restricted by ordinary statutory provisions.
Advisory Jurisdiction Art 143 President seeks opinion of SC. Clause 1: Public question (SC may refuse). Clause 2: Pre-constitution disputes (SC bound to answer). SC opinion is advisory only (not binding on President, not law under Art 141).
Part 3: The 5 Prerogative Writs (Art 32 vs Art 226) High-Frequency Question Area
Comparison Parameter Article 32 (Supreme Court) Article 226 (High Court)
Nature of Right Itself a Fundamental Right (Part III). SC cannot refuse remedy. Called the "Heart and Soul of the Constitution" by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar. A Constitutional Discretionary Remedy. HC may refuse if an alternative efficacious remedy exists.
Scope of Enforcement Narrower: Can be issued ONLY for enforcement of Fundamental Rights. Wider: Can be issued for Fundamental Rights AND for "any other legal purpose".
Territorial Reach Throughout the entire territory of India. Within its state territory (or where cause of action arises).
The 5 Writs Decoded in 1 Line:

1. Habeas Corpus ("To have the body"): Remedy against unlawful detention. Can be issued against both State and private individuals.

2. Mandamus ("We command"): Directs a public official, tribunal, or government to perform a mandatory statutory duty. Cannot be issued against the President or Governors.

3. Prohibition ("To forbid"): Issued to an inferior court or quasi-judicial body to prevent it from exceeding its jurisdiction (preventive in nature).

4. Certiorari ("To be certified / to quash"): Issued to an inferior court or tribunal to quash an order already passed without jurisdiction or in violation of natural justice (curative & corrective).

5. Quo-Warranto ("By what authority"): Inquires into the legality of a person holding a substantive public office. Any citizen can apply (relaxed *locus standi*).

⚠️ Examiner's Trap #2: Which Court Has Wider Writ Jurisdiction?

The High Court (Art 226) has a WIDER writ jurisdiction than the Supreme Court (Art 32) because the HC can issue writs for ordinary legal rights as well as fundamental rights, whereas the SC can ONLY issue writs for fundamental rights.

📜 Module 2: Supreme Court Rules, 2013

Framed under Article 145 of the Constitution. Regulates practice, Registry procedures, filing, and listing.

10 Marks Weightage
Part 1: The Registry & Powers of the Registrar Orders V & VI

The Registry is the administrative backbone of the Court. As a Junior Court Assistant, your daily duties tie directly into Registry workflows:

  • Registrar's Delegated Quasi-Judicial Powers (Order V): The Registrar sits in open court ("Registrar Court") to dispose of procedural matters:
    • Scrutinizing defective petitions and issuing defect notices.
    • Power to grant extension of time to cure defects up to a maximum of 28 days.
    • Ordering substituted service (publication in newspapers) when personal notice fails.
    • Summoning records from lower courts and tribunals.
    • Appeals against Registrar's order: Any person aggrieved by an order of the Registrar may appeal to a single Judge in Chambers within 15 Days (Order V Rule 3).
  • Matters Reserved for Judge in Chambers (Order VI): If a procedural matter exceeds the Registrar's power, it is listed before a single Judge in Chambers:
    • Applications for leave to appeal as an indigent person (in forma pauperis).
    • Application for exemption from surrendering (in criminal appeals).
    • Substitution of legal representatives in pending appeals.
Part 2: Crucial Filing Rules & Timelines Orders XV, XXI, XXII, XLVII
Procedure / Instrument Applicable Order Limitation / Time Window Critical Exam Rules
Caveat Order XV 90 Days from lodging Lodged to prevent ex-parte interim orders. Caveator must serve copy on the prospective petitioner.
Special Leave Petition (Civil) Order XXI 90 Days (or 60 Days) 90 days from HC decree; 60 days if HC refuses certificate of fitness.
Review Petition Order XLVII 30 Days Filed within 30 days of judgment. Heard by circulation in chambers without oral arguments. Accompanied by certificate of Advocate-on-Record.
Advocate-on-Record (AoR) Order IV Mandatory Representation Only an AoR can file appearance or act for a party. Senior Advocates CANNOT file vakalatnama or appear without an AoR.
Defect Curing Notice Order VIII 28 Days Petitioner given 28 days to cure Registry objections before petition is treated as defective.
⚠️ Examiner's Trap #3: Review Petition Oral Hearings

Exam question: "In the Supreme Court, a Review Petition is normally heard in open court by oral arguments. True or False?"
FALSE! Under Order XLVII Rule 3, Review Petitions are disposed of by circulation in Judges' Chambers without oral arguments, unless the Court specially directs an oral hearing.

👔 Module 3: Supreme Court Officers & Servants Rules, 1961

Framed under Article 146(2) of the Constitution by the Chief Justice of India. Governs the JCA service cadre.

10 Marks Weightage
Part 1: Constitutional Source & Independence Article 146 Anchor

• Sole Authority of CJI (Art 146(1)): Appointments of officers and servants of the Supreme Court are made exclusively by the Chief Justice of India or such other Judge or officer directed by him.

• Complete Independence from UPSC: The Union Public Service Commission (UPSC) has no consultation role in recruiting Supreme Court staff, unless the President by rule requires it (which is not mandated).

• Cadre Classification: Posts are divided into Class I (Gazetted), Class II (Gazetted / Non-Gazetted), Class III, and Class IV. The post of Junior Court Assistant (JCA) is a Class II / Group B Non-Gazetted post.

• Probation: Direct recruits to the post of JCA serve a mandatory probation period of 2 Years, extendable by the Appointing Authority.

Part 2: Conduct, Confidentiality & Disciplinary Actions The Rules Matrix

• Absolute Duty of Confidentiality: No officer or servant of the Court shall, except in accordance with general or special orders of the CJI, communicate directly or indirectly any official document, draft judgment, or roster information to any unauthorized person.

• Political Neutrality: Court servants are strictly barred from membership in, or association with, any political movement, political party, or standing for political elections.

• Classification of Penalties: A high-yield MCQ trap tests the exact statutory difference between Minor Penalties and Major Penalties:

Penalty Category Statutory Penalties Included Exam Trap Distinction
Minor Penalties 1. Censure (formal written reprimand)
2. Withholding of promotion for a specified period
3. Recovery from pay of whole or part of pecuniary loss caused to Court
4. Withholding of increments of pay WITHOUT cumulative effect 		Withholding increments without cumulative effect is a Minor penalty.
Major Penalties 1. Withholding of increments of pay WITH cumulative effect
2. Reduction to a lower post, grade, or lower stage in time-scale
3. Compulsory Retirement
4. Removal from Service (does not disqualify from future govt employment)
5. Dismissal from Service (ordinarily disqualifies from future govt employment) 		Removal vs Dismissal: Dismissal carries the bar for future government jobs; Removal does NOT disqualify.

• Appellate Authority & Limitation: Any appeal against an order imposing a penalty lies directly to the Chief Justice of India, whose decision is final and conclusive. The appeal must be preferred within 45 Days from the date of communication of the penalty order (Rule 15).

• Suspension & Subsistence Allowance: A member of the staff may be placed under suspension by the Appointing Authority pending departmental inquiry. During suspension, they are entitled to a Subsistence Allowance (normally 50% of leave salary) and cannot leave station without permission.

⚠️ Examiner's Trap #4: Removal vs Dismissal

Exam question: "Which of the following penalties disqualifies a person from future employment under the Government?"
Answer: Dismissal from service (Removal from service does NOT disqualify from future government employment).
