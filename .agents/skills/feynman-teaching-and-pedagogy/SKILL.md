# The Feynman Technique & Pedagogical Design

This skill translates complex, abstract, or intimidating concepts into intuitive, unforgettable explanations using Richard Feynman's principles of radical clarity and cognitive scaffolding.

## The 4-Step Feynman Technique

### Step 1: Choose the Target Concept & Define the Scope
- Name the exact idea, mechanism, or principle to be understood.
- Write the concept at the top of a blank page.

### Step 2: Explain It to a 12-Year-Old (Radical Simplification)
- Explain the concept using only words a bright 12-year-old would know.
- **Rule of Zero Jargon**: Whenever a technical or academic term is tempted, force yourself to describe what is actually happening in physical or relatable terms.
- **Anchor with Everyday Analogies**: Compare abstract systems to tangible physical realities (e.g., comparing inflation to adding water to orange juice concentrate).

### Step 3: Identify the Knowledge Gaps (The Friction Points)
- Where did the explanation become vague, hand-waving, or reliant on specialized vocabulary?
- Return to source material, foundational books, or first principles to genuinely understand the specific mechanics behind that gap.

### Step 4: Refine, Polish & Weave into a Narrative
- Rebuild the explanation as a coherent, engaging story with cause-and-effect transitions.
- Test the explanation with simple questions that verify true comprehension over mere rote memorization.

## Pedagogical Lesson Design Framework

When creating a lesson, guide, or course module, follow this cognitive sequence:

```text
1. The Curiosity Hook  ──►  2. Intuitive Analogy  ──►  3. The Formal Principle  ──►  4. Worked Example  ──►  5. Active Retrieval Check
```

1. **The Curiosity Hook**: Present a counter-intuitive paradox, mystery, or real-world problem that demands an explanation.
2. **Intuitive Analogy**: Introduce a visual or physical metaphor that bridges the unknown to something the learner already understands.
3. **The Formal Principle**: Introduce the precise definition, terminology, or rules now that the mental scaffolding is established.
4. **Worked Example**: Walk through a concrete case step-by-step, vocalizing the internal decision-making at every stage.
5. **Active Retrieval & Application**: Provide an exercise or Socratic prompt that requires the learner to reconstruct the concept themselves.
