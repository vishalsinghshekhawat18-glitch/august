# Storytelling & Narrative Craft

This skill elevates writing by transforming dry descriptions into immersive, emotionally resonant narratives that grip readers from the first sentence.

## Core Narrative Principles

### 1. "Show, Don't Tell" (Sensory Immersion)
- **Telling**: "He was nervous before going on stage."
- **Showing**: "His collar felt two sizes too tight. Behind the curtain, he wiped his palms against his wool trousers three times before checking his watch again."
- **Sensory Palette**: Incorporate at least two non-visual senses per major scene (scent, texture, ambient temperature, acoustic reverberation).

### 2. Micro-Tension on Every Page
Every scene requires an unanswered question or a subtle friction:
- **Internal Tension**: A clash between what the protagonist wants vs. what they need, or fear of exposure.
- **Interpersonal Tension**: Subtext in dialogue—what characters leave unsaid.
- **Situational Tension**: Ticking clocks, shifting environments, or impending confrontations.

### 3. Pacing & Time Control
- **Expand Emotional Peaks**: When high-stakes events happen (a revelation, betrayal, or epiphanic moment), slow down time. Detail the micro-seconds, thoughts, physical reactions, and sensory shifts.
- **Compress Transitions**: Compress routine travel, mundane logistics, or days of uneventful waiting into a single sentence ("Two weeks passed in silence").

### 4. Dialogue with Subtext
- Avoid "on-the-nose" dialogue where characters say exactly what they feel.
- Give each speaker distinct cadence, vocabulary, and defense mechanisms.
- Strip away narrative dialogue tags ("he said angrily") in favor of action beats that demonstrate emotion.

## Story Structure Framework

```text
Status Quo  ──►  Inciting Incident  ──►  Progressive Complications  ──►  Crisis / Climax  ──►  Resolution / New Normal
```

1. **Status Quo**: Ground the reader in the protagonist's ordinary world, worldview, and unfulfilled desire.
2. **Inciting Incident**: An unexpected catalyst disrupts equilibrium and forces a decision.
3. **Progressive Complications**: Obstacles mount; simple solutions fail; stakes escalate.
4. **Crisis Point**: The character is forced to make an agonizing choice between two irreconcilable goods or the lesser of two evils.
5. **Climax & Resolution**: The choice is executed; the transformation is tested; a new equilibrium is established.
