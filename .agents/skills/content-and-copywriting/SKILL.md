# Content Creation & Copywriting

This skill guides the creation of engaging, reader-centric content that captures attention, delivers immediate value, and drives action.

## Core Copywriting Frameworks

When drafting content, select the framework that matches the objective:

### 1. PAS (Problem – Agitate – Solution)
*Best for persuasive articles, landing page copy, and direct-response emails.*
1. **Problem**: Pinpoint a specific friction, pain point, or frustration the reader experiences.
2. **Agitate**: Vividly illustrate the hidden costs, emotional toll, or compounding risk of ignoring the problem.
3. **Solution**: Present the clear, straightforward resolution or offer.

### 2. AIDA (Attention – Interest – Desire – Action)
*Best for promotional pieces, newsletters, and announcements.*
1. **Attention**: Irresistible headline or opening hook.
2. **Interest**: Surprising insights, relatable stories, or counter-intuitive facts.
3. **Desire**: Paint the picture of life after applying the advice/solution.
4. **Action**: Single, frictionless Call to Action (CTA).

### 3. The 4 U's (for Headlines & Subject Lines)
Every headline must balance:
- **Urgent**: Gives a reason to read now.
- **Unique**: Distinct angle not heard everywhere.
- **Ultra-specific**: Numbers, exact timelines, clear nouns.
- **Useful**: Concrete benefit to the reader.

## Content Anatomy & Rules

1. **The Hook (First 2 Lines)**:
   - Make a bold contrarian claim, state a provocative statistic, or ask a high-stakes question.
   - Avoid generic introductions ("In today's fast-paced world...").

2. **Body & Scannability**:
   - Short sentences (averaging 12–18 words).
   - Paragraphs no longer than 3–4 lines.
   - Use sub-headers that tell the story even if someone only reads the headings.
   - Bullet points for lists with bolded lead-ins.

3. **Call To Action (CTA)**:
   - Provide one single, direct next step (e.g., "Read the full case study", "Reply with your thoughts", "Sign up below").
   - Avoid multiple competing calls to action.
