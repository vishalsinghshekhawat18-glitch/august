# Strategic Decision Frameworks & Mental Models

This skill equips the agent to structure ambiguous, high-stakes decisions using proven mental models to prevent cognitive biases and uncover blind spots.

## Mental Models Toolkit

Select the framework appropriate for the decision at hand:

### 1. First Principles Thinking
*Best for breaking through conventional wisdom or redesigning processes from scratch.*
1. **Identify Assumptions**: State the current standard beliefs or industry norms.
2. **Deconstruct to Fundamental Truths**: What are the indisputable, foundational facts that cannot be broken down further?
3. **Reconstruct Solutions**: Build a new solution ground-up solely from fundamental truths.

### 2. SWOT & TOWS Matrix
*Best for strategic positioning and situational analysis.*
- **Strengths (Internal)**: Unique assets, capabilities, relationships.
- **Weaknesses (Internal)**: Resource deficits, skill gaps, vulnerabilities.
- **Opportunities (External)**: Emerging trends, market shifts, regulatory changes.
- **Threats (External)**: Competitor moves, macroeconomic friction, technology obsolescence.
- **TOWS Action Matrix**:
  - *S-O*: Use internal strengths to capitalize on external opportunities.
  - *W-O*: Overcome weaknesses by pursuing opportunities.
  - *S-T*: Use strengths to shield against external threats.
  - *W-T*: Minimize weaknesses and avoid external threats.

### 3. MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive)
*Best for breaking complex problems into sub-problems without overlap or gaps.*
- Deconstruct problems into distinct branches where no two categories overlap (Mutually Exclusive), and all possibilities together cover the entire scope (Collectively Exhaustive).

### 4. Second-Order Thinking ("And Then What?")
*Best for forecasting ripple effects and preventing short-term optimizations that harm long-term viability.*
- **First-Order**: Immediate, obvious result of the decision.
- **Second-Order**: The systemic reaction, behavioral adaptations, or competitor countermeasures triggered by the first-order result.
- **Third-Order**: The compounding long-term outcome across 12–36 months.

### 5. Type 1 vs. Type 2 Decisions (Bezos Door Analogy)
- **Type 1 (One-Way Doors)**: Irreversible or catastrophic to reverse. Require slow, deliberate, multi-stakeholder vetting.
- **Type 2 (Two-Way Doors)**: Reversible with low cost. Prioritize speed, rapid iteration, and immediate experimentation over lengthy consensus.
