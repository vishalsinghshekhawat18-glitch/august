1. Findings

1 · The marks blueprint is fixed RPSC gives each unit almost the same marks every cycle. Accounting, Law, Management and Sociology get about 20 marks each every time (18–21). Ethics has held 65–70 since it moved to Paper II in 2018, Science and Technology 60–71, Public Administration 53–64 and Indian Polity 33–42 (table 2). Time can be split by unit weight from day one. Nothing in a unit is "optional".
2 · A core of lines comes back every cycle 31 lines of the 2026 syllabus were asked in all 5 cycles. The heaviest are SC3 (Biology), SC1 (Chemistry), ET6 (Moral thinkers and philosophers of India and the world), PA10 (State administration), SC2 (Physics), RE6 (Infrastructure), PO1 (Constituent Assembly, influences, philosophy; Fundamental Rights, DPSP, Fundamental Duties), SC5 (Indian scientists and institutions; robotics, nano, quantum; S&T in India and Rajasthan; policies; Digital India; cyber security). Notes for these get the most depth and the most answer practice (table 3).
3 · 19 lines have never been asked, mostly because they are new in 2026 Those include Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita and BNSS 2023, GST, accounting standards, computerised accounting, e-governance, leadership theories, workplace communication and ethics case studies (list 4). Old papers cannot show how RPSC will frame these, so they are covered from the syllabus and current material. They are where a well-prepared candidate pulls ahead of one who only drilled old papers.
4 · Short questions are recall questions 330 of the 681 GS questions are 2-mark, 15-word answers. 66% of those open with what/who/which, define, or name/list/mention. They test exact facts: names, years, sections, districts, definitions. 10-mark questions mostly ask to discuss, describe or explain (table 5). Notes need a precise fact layer, and the answer-writing drills need two separate formats.
5 · About a sixth to a quarter of GS marks carry a Rajasthan angle Questions that name Rajasthan or sit on a Rajasthan-only line took 16–24% of the 600 GS marks in each cycle (table 6). On top of that, many India-level questions reward a Rajasthan example. Every notebook keeps a Rajasthan box per topic.
6 · 7–16% of old marks are on topics the 2026 syllabus dropped These are mostly old accounting and finance theory, personality and psychology theory, older administration topics, and in 2016 the whole Reasoning unit (table 7). Old questions on these lines are not practised. Their marks move to the new lines in finding 3.
7 · The pattern itself moves a little Paper II in 2024 had 18 five-mark questions where earlier cycles had 14 (table 8). The 2026 scheme should be checked against the first paper set under it, and mocks should follow the newest pattern, not an average.

12. Method and limits

  • Source: scans of the RPSC question papers published by Adda247, read page by page. The English version is recorded (RPSC treats it as standard), except for Hindi-only Paper IV items.
